**What this is.** For each target, the Earth places whose **terrain shape** (slope, roughness, relief, curvature at a ~926 m grid) and **yearly sunlight regime** best match it. Score = 0.6 × terrain_sim + 0.4 × light_sim (mode `terrain+light`). It says nothing about geology, chemistry, regolith, temperature or access. These are hypotheses for field scouting, not recommendations.

Method: the 150 best terrain matches after 50 km non-maximum suppression are re-ranked by score; light_sim and the window come from the 28-day best-window search of the analytic light model (docs/light.md). "known" = within 100 km of a site in `engine/known_analogs.yaml`; "new" = not.

Reading the numbers: terrain_sim and light_sim are each 0–1 (1 = identical). Lunar polar targets have a Sun that never rises more than ~1.5° above a flat horizon; no Earth site reproduces that, and light_sim only measures how close the 28-day elevation histogram gets (the best windows are local-winter months).

Per-target ranges of terrain_sim among the listed candidates: Shackleton rim 0.39–0.47; Malapert Massif 0.15–0.21; Site finder #1 0.49–0.65; Jezero crater 0.70–0.83; Gale crater 0.38–0.62; Oxia Planum 0.88–0.91. Low values mean even the best Earth terrain is a weak shape match for that target.
